When someone dies, several institutions owe your family money. None of them will tell you about the others.

The bank asks for one set of papers. The insurer asks for another. The EPFO office wants three separate forms, and only mentions the second one after you have queued for the first. Each counter reveals its own list, and only when you are standing at it.

So claims are filed late, filed wrong, or abandoned. Money sits unclaimed in accounts and EPFO ledgers for years, and the person least equipped to do bureaucracy is the one who has to do all of it.

What you are handed today

Six counters, six lists, no index. Every one of these is real, and each is discovered separately.

Bank branch

No nominee on the account

Annex I-B claim form signed by every claimant, bond of indemnity I-C, disclaimer I-D from heirs who are not claiming, legal heir certificate or affidavit I-E.

Life insurer

Death claim

The original policy document. If it is lost, apply for a duplicate first, which is its own process.

EPFO office

Three separate forms

Form 20 for the provident fund. Form 10D for the survivor pension. Form 5IF for the death insurance — and nobody mentions that one.

Employer HR

Gratuity and final salary

Legal heir proof, plus whatever nominee record the employer happens to hold on file.

Nadakacheri

Legal heir certificate

Needed before several of the lists above will be accepted. Weeks, not days.

City civil court

Succession certificate

Only if a balance crosses ₹15 lakh with no nominee. A petition under s.372, an asset schedule, NOCs from co-heirs. Months.

The one almost nobody files

EDLI

Employees’ Deposit Linked Insurance

If the person who died held a salaried job, the EPFO owes the family a death-insurance payout on top of the provident fund balance. It is close to automatic. It needs one form.

Most families never hear about it, because the counter they are standing at is not the counter that pays it.

How it works

01

It asks, you answer

One question at a time, spoken aloud in Kannada or English. No forms to read, no jargon. It never asks about a provident fund if there was never a salaried job.

02

Rules derive the claims

Your answers run through a fixed table built from the RBI 2025 deceased-claims directions, the Hindu Succession Act, and the EPFO forms. The language model never decides what you are owed.

03

You get the register

A numbered schedule that fills in as you talk: where each claim is filed, which form it needs, and which documents you do not have yet. Print it and take it with you.
